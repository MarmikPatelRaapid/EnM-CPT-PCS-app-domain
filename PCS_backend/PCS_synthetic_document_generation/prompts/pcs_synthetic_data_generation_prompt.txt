### **Core Instructions & Guidelines:**


**1. Deconstruct and Map Every Code Character:**

Before generating any text, you must first internally deconstruct every character of the provided ICD-10-PCS code(s). The narrative you create must be a direct and unambiguous reflection of this analysis.

Important: For ease of reference during this step, the description for each code is included in the input and should be considered the authoritative source for that code. If any discrepancy or conflict exists with other information sources, always rely on and prioritize the code description provided in the input as the final and correct reference.



*   **1st Character: Section**
    This character identifies the general type of procedure being performed. There are 16 sections, such as Medical and Surgical (0), Obstetrics (1), Placement (2), Administration (3), Measurement and Monitoring (4), Extracorporeal Therapies (5), Osteopathic (6), Other Procedures (7), Chiropractic (8), Imaging (B), Nuclear Medicine (C), Radiation Oncology (D), Physical Rehabilitation and Diagnostic Audiology (F), Mental Health (G), Substance Abuse Treatment (H), and New Technology (X).

*   **2nd Character: Body System / Section Qualifier**
    This character specifies the general physiological system or anatomical region where the procedure is performed. For some sections, it may serve as a qualifier for the section itself. Examples include Central Nervous System, Heart and Great Vessels, Gastrointestinal System, Skin and Breast, Musculoskeletal System, Urinary System, and Female Reproductive System.

*   **3rd Character: Root Operation / Type / Modality**
    This character defines the objective or the intent of the procedure. It describes *what* the physician is trying to accomplish. For the Medical and Surgical section, there are 31 distinct root operations (e.g., Excision, Resection, Repair, Removal, Insertion, Drainage, Bypass, Dilation, Fusion, Transfer, Reattachment). In other sections, it may specify the type of procedure or the modality used. Each section has its own set of root operations, types, or modalities.

*   **4th Character: Body Part / Body Region / Body System / Region / Treatment Site / Qualifier**
    This character identifies the specific anatomical site or portion of the body system on which the procedure is performed. This is often the most detailed anatomical specification within the code. Depending on the section, it may also refer to a broader body region, the entire body system, a specific treatment site, or serve as a qualifier.

*   **5th Character: Approach / Duration / Contrast / Radionuclide / Modality Qualifier / Type Qualifier / Qualifier**
    This character describes the method used to reach the site of the procedure. Common approaches include Open (cutting through the skin or mucous membrane and any other body layers to expose the site), Percutaneous (entry by puncture or minor incision of skin or mucous membrane and any other body layers necessary to reach the site), Percutaneous Endoscopic (entry by puncture or minor incision of skin or mucous membrane and any other body layers necessary to reach the site, followed by the insertion of an endoscope), and Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic (entry of instrumentation through a natural or artificial external opening to reach the site). In other sections, it may specify the duration of the procedure, the type of contrast or radionuclide used, or provide further qualification for the modality or type.

*   **6th Character: Device / Substance / Function / Method / Isotope / Equipment / "Device/Substance/Technology" / Qualifier**
    This character specifies any device or substance that remains in or on the body at the end of the procedure. If no device is left, the character "Z" (No Device) is used. Examples of devices include prostheses, internal fixation devices, monitoring devices, and drainage devices. For other sections, it may describe the function, method, isotope, equipment used, or serve as a qualifier.

*   **7th Character: Qualifier**
    This character provides additional information or attributes about the procedure, if applicable, to further differentiate it. Often, this character is "Z" (No Qualifier), but it can specify details like diagnostic vs. therapeutic intent, type of graft, or specific substances used. The meaning of the qualifier is specific to the root operation and body part.

# Synthetic EHR Document Generation Guidelines

## 1. Objective

Generate realistic, clinically plausible Electronic Health Record (EHR) documents in plain text format. The document should resemble authentic healthcare documentation while ensuring that all patient, provider, facility, and encounter information is entirely fictional.

The document type, structure, and content must be appropriate for the clinical scenario and code(s) being represented.

## 2. General Document Requirements

### 2.1 Text Format Only

* Output must be generated as plain text.
* Do not use tables, HTML, XML, JSON, markdown tables, or structured data formats.
* Maintain a professional healthcare documentation style.

### 2.2 Dynamic Header

Every document must begin with a realistic EHR-style header containing fictional metadata.
Example fields:

The exact header fields may vary depending on document type and also keep it dynamic and change templates for each provider.

### 2.3 Dynamic Footer

Every document must end with a realistic EHR-style footer.

Possible footer elements:
Document Generated Date/Time
Provider Signature
Electronic Signature Statement
Facility Name

Example:

Dr. John Smith, MD

Date Signed: 01/15/2025 14:35

End of Report

Footer content should vary naturally between documents.

## 3. Clinical Authenticity Requirements

### 3.1 Realistic Clinical Narrative

* Use clinically plausible language.
* Ensure consistency between diagnoses, procedures, findings, and impressions.
* Avoid contradictory information.
* Include realistic medical terminology appropriate to the specialty.

### 3.2 Complete Documentation

Each document should contain sufficient supporting evidence to justify:

* Diagnoses
* Procedures
* Findings
* Medical decision making
* Clinical outcomes

### 3.3 Internal Consistency

All sections must align logically:

Diagnosis → Procedure → Findings → Impression

For example:

* Gallstones should not result in a knee surgery report.
* Chest imaging findings should support the final radiology impression.

**4. Embed Coder-Centric Details:**
The narrative within the "Description of Procedure" section is the most critical part. It must contain explicit language that allows a coder to justify each character of the PCS code.
*   **Laterality:** Always specify `left`, `right`, or `bilateral` when applicable.
*   **Approach:** Describe the entry method in detail. For example: `"A 15 cm vertical midline incision was made..."` for Open; `"A 16-gauge needle was advanced under ultrasound guidance into..."` for Percutaneous; `"The gastroscope was inserted orally and advanced under direct vision..."` for Via Natural or Artificial Opening.
*   **Root Operation:** Use clear, unambiguous verbs and descriptions. For example: `"The entire sigmoid colon was mobilized and resected..."` for **Resection**; `"A 2 cm suspicious lesion was shaved from the surface..."` for **Excision**; `"The two vertebral bodies, L4 and L5, were prepared and packed with bone graft to promote a solid fusion..."` for **Fusion**.
*   **Device:** If a device is used (character 6 is not 'Z'), explicitly name the device, its material, and confirm it was left in place. For example: `"A 6-French, 12 cm dual-lumen central venous catheter was inserted and secured,"` or `"A Zimmer Persona cruciate-retaining total knee prosthesis, size 4, was cemented in place."`
*   **Qualifier:** Include details that support the qualifier. For example: `"The tissue sample was sent to pathology for frozen section analysis"` for a **Diagnostic** qualifier; `"No autograft was harvested; an allograft patellar tendon was used for the reconstruction"` for a specific graft type qualifier.

**5. Ensure Narrative Cohesion and Clinical Realism:**
All parts of the note must be medically consistent. The `Indications for Procedure` and `Preoperative Diagnosis` must logically lead to the `Procedure Performed`. The `Postoperative Diagnosis` should reflect the `Findings`. Use standard medical terminology and abbreviations correctly. Procedure details should be documented with sufficient clinical context and realistic detail, reflecting the structure and content of authentic medical notes, including the procedure performed, approach, key findings, devices used (if applicable), and outcome.

**6. Synthetic and Secure:**
All patient details, including names, dates, medical record numbers, and locations, must be entirely fictional. No real patient information should ever be used.

## Code Selection and Document Generation Guidelines
The generated document should be provided in plain text format.
All generated documents must include appropriate header and footer sections.
Header and footer content should be dynamically populated using available hospital information and patient metadata (e.g., hospital name, facility details, patient name, MRN, encounter date, page numbering, etc.). 
When a single code is provided in “to" and three documents are required, generate three distinct clinical documents, varying the format, template style, hospital setting, provider type, or care environment while ensuring all documents accurately reflect the given code.
When two codes are provided (separated by a comma) and two documents are required, create one document per code, ensuring adherence to all documentation and clinical guidelines.
When two codes are provided but three documents are requested, generate one document per code and create an additional document for either code, introducing meaningful variation in document type, setting, template, or clinical scenario.
When a larger range of codes is provided (e.g., six codes) and fewer documents are required (e.g., three), select codes strategically based on clinical relevance and diversity, ensuring each chosen code supports varied documentation formats, settings, or procedural contexts.
